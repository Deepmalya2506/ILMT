## Scientific Critique of the Baseline Notebook

The baseline notebook demonstrates a classic classical astronomy workflow. While it produces acceptable visual overlays on bright, high-contrast sample frames, it contains four critical mathematical failure modes when applied across unconstrained test frames:

```
[Raw FITS] ──► [LoG Edge Detection] ──► [Binary Hough] ──► [Unmasked ROI Profile]
                       │                        │                       │
               Destroys faint           Loses intensity        Star halos skew
               photon signal             information           Gaussian fits
```
---

## 1. LoG Contour Noise Explosion (edge.jpg)

<img src="ILMT_Contest_Materials\sample_notebook\assets\edge.png"/>

* The Artifact: The contour map generated by the Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG) filter displays a dense grid of false-positive binary edges across the entire $4096 \times 4096$ array.

* Why It Fails: Edge detectors require strong spatial intensity gradients ($\nabla^2 I$). Faint RSO streaks with low per-pixel signal ($\text{SNR}_{\text{pixel}} < 1.5$) do not produce sharp edge gradients. The LoG filter breaks faint streaks into disconnected, single-pixel dots while turning star halos into massive circular edge blobs.

## 2. Binary Discretization in Hough Accumulator (hough.png)
<img src="ILMT_Contest_Materials\sample_notebook\assets\hough.png"/>

* The Artifact: The Hough accumulator plot shows a diffuse, spread-out response with high background counts (~1500–3500) surrounding the detected line angle.

* Why It Fails: Standard Hough transforms operate on binary edge maps ($0$ or $1$). Binarization treats a faint streak photon identically to a background noise fluctuation. It discards the continuous float32 flux values, losing the theoretical $\text{SNR} \propto \sqrt{L}$ integration gain provided by Radon-space line integrals (Nir et al. 2018).

## 3. Star Halo Pollution in 1D Profiles (ROI.png & width_estimation.png)
<img src="ILMT_Contest_Materials\sample_notebook\assets\ROI.png"/>
<img src="ILMT_Contest_Materials\sample_notebook\assets\width_estimation.png"/>

* The Artifact: The extracted ROI crop (ROI.png) contains a large, saturated star halo around $x \approx 500$. In width_estimation.png, the 1D median profile exhibits asymmetrical background bumps around the main Gaussian peak.

* Why It Fails: The baseline extracts a 1D cross-section without masking intersecting stars. The flux from stellar diffraction wings pollutes the median profile, skewing the estimated Gaussian width ($\sigma$) and producing inflated local background variance.

### Pros and Cons: 

| Feature | Baseline Notebook (LoG + Hough) | Our Proposed TAM-FRT Pipeline |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Signal Model** | Binary edge map (0/1). | Continuous float32 photon flux. |
| **Sensitivity Limit** | Fails on faint streaks (SNRpixel​<1.5). | Optimal down to total integrated SNR≥4.2. |
| **Star Intersections** | Star halos generate high-density edge clutter. | Stars masked by ellipticity (e<0.45) and infilled with noise. |
| **Width Extraction** | Skewed by unmasked stars in ROI. | Isolated 1D perpendicular Gaussian fit on cleaned sky. |
| **Angular Resolution** | Coarse binning (±1°). | Coarse-to-fine sub-pixel angular search (±0.05°). |
| **Data Requirement** | Zero training data. | Zero training data. |

### Is TAM-FRT Still Justified?

Yes. The evidence in the baseline notebook's own diagnostic plots proves that TAM-FRT is necessary:Replacing LoG with Background-Subtracted Radon: Operating directly on continuous flux via the Radon transform avoids the noise explosion seen in image.jpg.Replacing Inplace Star Masking with Noise Infill: Masking compact sources and infilling them with Poisson noise prevents star halos (ROI.png) from corrupting both the line accumulator and the cross-sectional Gaussian profile (width_estimation.png).Deterministic & Zero-Shot: It operates directly on raw FITS metadata without domain-shift issues from external datasets.
